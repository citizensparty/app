How it works

Power starts in your neighborhood.

BC is divided into grids. You belong to the one you live in, and that's where you have your say.

1

Join your grid

Pick your grid, the part of BC where you live, and you're in. It's free.

2

Propose

Have an idea for what the party should stand for? Put it forward. If your neighbors back it, the whole grid votes on it.

3

Vote

Every member has the right to vote on everything, and the responsibility to show up when it counts.

4

Go BC-wide

A principle that passes in one grid can go to every grid. If most grids agree, it becomes the party's.

Our rules

Built so nobody can take it over.

Most parties are run by a board and an annual meeting that a few people can capture. Ours is written so that can't happen.

Members are the final say

No board or leader can overrule what the members decide.

Nobody can be pushed out

No leader or board can expel a member.

Recall is always open

Anyone elected can be removed, but only by the members who elected them.

Everything in the open

Party communications are public. There's no inner circle and no private channel.

No fees, ever

Membership is free. Your voice doesn't depend on your wallet.

No off switch

The votes aren't kept in one place. Members keep copies on their own computers, so no one can shut it down.

Most constitutions are written on paper. Ours is built into the app.See how the rules enforce themselves, who holds what power, the risks, and the backup plan if something goes wrong. Read our constitution →
Your vote

No one can change your vote. Not even us.

In most parties, a few people count the votes and everyone else takes their word for it. Here, you don't have to take anyone's word for it.

  • You can always find your own vote. Your vote is listed under a private code that only you know. If anyone ever changed it, you'd see it.
  • The party doesn't keep the votes. Members do. Copies of every vote are kept by members on their own computers, so the party can never change a result.
  • Your vote is locked before it leaves your phone. Your phone locks your vote with a key only you have. Even if everyone else agreed to change it, it wouldn't match, and you'd know.

The app's code will be public, so anyone can check how it works.

FAQ

Questions

Does it cost anything to join?

No. Membership is free and always will be. If the party ever raises money for a campaign, giving will be voluntary and the budget will be public.

Who can join?

During the beta, anyone can join. In the final version, membership will be open to Canadian citizens who live in British Columbia.

How will you make sure people use their real address?

In the final version, your address will be checked against your driver's license by a trusted outside ID service. During the beta, we take your word for it.

A driver's license doesn't prove citizenship, does it?

Correct. We're looking at ways to also use a citizenship card or a passport.

Why a grid?

BC is divided into squares of equal size, about 120 of them, and you belong to the one you live in. The lines follow simple math, not politics, so nobody can redraw them to help one side win. Each one is a local area, so you decide things alongside people from your own part of the province.

Where is the constitution?

This is a beta, so for now the app's rules are how the constitution is expressed. Once we've worked out the details, we'll write it down in full, and we may put it to a members' vote before it's adopted.

Can people see how I voted?

Votes are listed under a private code, never your name. Only you can see which code is yours. Nobody else, not even the party, can tell how you voted.

Do you use Tor?

Yes, for ballots, in the app. Tor hides which internet connection a message came from. In the Android app, your ballot travels through Tor, so not even the computer that receives it can tell where it came from. Sending a vote takes a few seconds longer; everything else stays just as fast. On a computer, you get the same protection by opening the app in Tor Browser. It's coming to the iPhone app too.

Why do I have to vote?

Because a party run by its members only works if the members show up. Skip a required vote and you sit out the next 72 hours. Optional votes, like BC-wide ones, never count against you.

Who runs it?

The members. There's no board or head office that can override them. Anyone elected answers to the grid that elected them and can be recalled by it.

Can I change my grid?

Yes, any time, in the app's Settings. Moving locks your account for 72 hours: you can still look around, but you can't propose or vote. That stops people from looking for proposals they like in other areas and moving in just to vote on them. During the beta, this wait is waived. In the final version, we plan to confirm where you live with an outside ID check, so your grid matches your home.

What if someone posts too many proposals?

Anyone can propose as many principles as they like. We don't block or limit members, because deciding who gets silenced is a power nobody should have. Instead, you can ignore anyone who posts too much: their proposals disappear from your lists while they gather support. If one gets enough support to go to a full vote, it shows up again, because every member votes on it. Ignoring is private, and it's saved only on your phone.

Can I see the code?

Yes. All of it is public on GitHub: the app, the host program and this website. Anyone can read it, check that it does what we say, or build their own app on the same record.

Is this the final version?

Not yet. We're still testing. Votes during testing are for practice and won't become party policy. Before the official launch, we may start over with a clean slate. If we do, you'll just sign up again. Once we launch, every vote is kept for good, and no one can erase it, not even us.

How do I join?

Get the app on your phone or computer, enter your address to find your grid, and you're in. It's free.
